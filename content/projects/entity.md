## Project Overview
The core goal of this project was to design an experience that makes users feel genuinely uncomfortable and weird. We shaped this atmosphere by centering the interaction around an idol that constantly "watches" the participant while they solving the puzzle.

Instead of giving clear written instructions, we used light and sound cues to guide participants to complete the process step by step. The interaction builds up to a climax where they are forced to physically squeeze a tangible heart. By mixing the creepy feeling of being observed with the actual physical resistance of squeezing the heart, we wanted to turn a simple installation into an intense, ritualistic experience.

## Design Process
![Ideation](/image/Entity/moodBoard.jpg)


#### Concept: The Anxiety of Being Watched

Our ideation started with a simple question: **How do we design discomfort?** 

To capture this feeling, our team brainstormed using different mood boards. Mine focused on unsettling textures, old-style computer aesthetics, and the concept of "being watched." The team loved the creepy vibe of staring eyes, which became the core conceptual pillar for our idol.

#### From Concept to Altar

We translated the feeling of being watched into an interactive altar, with the digital idol at its centre. The surrounding objects and carefully arranged setup helped create a ritual-like atmosphere, making the experience feel unsettling as users approached the idol.
![Ideation](/image/Entity/sketch.jpg)
 
#### Designing the Physical Setup
I helped bring the altar to life by building the physical setup around the interactive elements. I designed and assembled bases for the salvaged electronics, organised the internal wiring, and worked on the scenography to make the installation feel like a cohesive ritual space rather than a prototype. I also made sure the digital idol’s gaze aligned with the user’s eye level.

#### The Light Guide (Prototype → Demo)
During our first prototype exhibition, users were confused about what to do next without clear instructions.

Rather than adding text that could break the immersive experience, we programmed dynamic LED lighting and sound cues to guide users’ attention towards the relevant artifacts and lead them through the interaction step by step.

#### From USB Cables to a Pumping Heart (Prototype → Demo)
Our initial idea was to have users plug a USB stick into the idol to “insert data” and awaken it. However, during testing, this interaction felt confusing and awkward to perform.

We replaced it with a squeezable, tangible heart. Squeezing the heart became a more intuitive way to bring the idol to life, while its texture and physical resistance made the interaction feel uncomfortable in a way that reinforced the dark narrative.

#### Making the Artifacts Discoverable (Demo → Final)
In the earlier setup, the artifacts were not clearly presented as objects users could pick up and interact with.

We added two tables beside the altar to display the artifacts separately. This made them easier to spot and access, helping users understand that they were part of the ritual and needed to be placed on the altar.

## Technical Specifications

<div style="text-align: center;">
  <img src="/image/Entity/software.jpg"
       alt="TouchDesigner and Pure Data"
       style="width: 65%; height: auto; border-radius: 8px;">
  <p style="font-size: 0.85em; color: #555; margin-top: 8px;">
    Behind the scenes: TouchDesigner (left) and Pure Data (right).
  </p>
</div>

*   **Compute & Control:** Laptops ×2, ESP32 microcontrollers ×2
*   **Software Stack:** TouchDesigner, Pure Data, Python, Chataigne
*   **Sensors & Input:** Kinect sensor, RFID scanners ×2 (with 5 cards), light-dependent resistor (LDR), force-sensitive resistor (FSR)
*   **Output & Feedback:** 1080p screens, speakers, independent NeoPixel LED strips, haptic motor
*   **Fabrication:** Cardboard altar structure, 3D-printed idol body parts, and custom artifacts assembled from salvaged electronic components.

## Contributions

* **Concept & Ideation:** Developed the initial mood board around staring eyes, retro technology, and unsettling textures, shaping the project’s digital idol concept.
* **Physical Prototyping:** Sourced salvaged electronic components and built the interactive artifacts, custom bases, and main table structure.
* **Assembly & Exhibition Setup (Collaborative):** Assembled the altar, managed cable routing, and sourced props to create an immersive environment for the final exhibition.
